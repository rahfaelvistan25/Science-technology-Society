# Section 7: Teacher-Guided Discussion – Questions to Ask During Class

## Discussion 1: Human Curiosity and Innovation
1. Why do you think early humans started creating tools?
2. What role did observation play in the development of early science?
3. Why was the discovery and controlled use of fire important?
4. How did agriculture contribute to the development of civilizations?
5. What do you think would happen if humans stopped developing new technologies?

### Facilitator Notes & Expected Student Takeaways:
- **Survival Imperative:** Toolmaking was born of physical vulnerability. Humans lack sharp claws or thick fur; tools were external physiological extensions.
- **Empiricism before Formal Science:** Systematic trial and error (e.g. noticing which berries poisoned comrades vs. which cured ailments) was proto-scientific observation.
- **Fire as Energy Catalyst:** Fire outsourced biological digestion (allowing brain encephalization) and enabled nocturnal cultural bonding.

## Discussion 2: Science and Technology in Modern Society
1. Can technology exist without science? Explain your answer.
2. How does science help engineers develop modern machines?
3. What modern technology has made the greatest difference in your daily life?
4. Does technology always make people's lives better? Why or why not?
5. What responsibilities should people have when using technology?

### Facilitator Notes & Socratic Probes:
- **Interdependence:** Historically, empirical craft technology preceded scientific theory (e.g., steam engines were built before thermodynamics was formalized). Today, cutting-edge technology (semiconductors, gene therapy) is deeply dependent on fundamental scientific research.
- **Dual Nature of Innovation:** Prompt students to examine negative externalities (social isolation, surveillance capitalism, e-waste, disinformation).

## Discussion 3: Critical Thinking – The Ancient Community Simulation
### Situation:
Imagine that you are living in an ancient civilization without electricity, computers, smartphones, or modern transportation.
Your community has the following problems:
- People have difficulty transporting food.
- Farmers depend on rainfall.
- Information is communicated through messengers.
- Food cannot be stored for long periods.
- People have limited medical knowledge.

### Prompt:
If you were an inventor during that period, what technology would you develop first to help your community?
1. What problem are you trying to solve?
2. What technology would you invent?
3. What materials would you need?
4. How would your invention improve people's lives?
5. How could your invention influence future generations?
