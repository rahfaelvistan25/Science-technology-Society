# Section 11: Teacher's Guide – Suggested Classroom Flow & Preview

## Suggested Classroom Flow (60–90 Minutes)

| Allocated Time | Teaching Activity | Pedagogical Focus |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **5 minutes** | Opening, attendance, and lesson motivation | Spark curiosity by asking students to name one technology they couldn't live without. |
| **10 minutes** | Introduction to historical antecedents | Define antecedents using the phone/communication historical progression. |
| **15 minutes** | Meaning of science and technology | Compare definitions, run the sunlight plant experiment example, review differences. |
| **15 minutes** | Origins of science and technology | Examine early human curiosity, Acheulean tools, fire benefits, Neolithic farming. |
| **15 minutes** | Contributions of ancient civilizations | Explore Mesopotamia, Egypt, Greece, China, and India using visual slides. |
| **10 minutes** | Relationship between science, technology, and society | Analyze the continuous STS cycle, smartphone case, and healthcare loop. |
| **10 minutes** | Classroom discussion and activities | Run Activity 1 (Classifier) and discuss the Ancient Inventor scenario. |
| **10 minutes** | Assessment and generalization | Administer the 15-question quiz, synthesize core takeaways, review key terms. |

## Preparation for Lesson 2: The Three Intellectual Revolutions

The next lesson will discuss the three major intellectual revolutions that fundamentally transformed humanity's understanding of the universe, life, and the human mind:

1. **The Copernican Revolution:** How humanity shifted from a geocentric to a heliocentric understanding of the universe.
2. **The Darwinian Revolution:** How humanity shifted from static creationism to an evolutionary understanding of biological species and natural selection.
3. **The Freudian Revolution:** How humanity began investigating the unconscious mind, ego, and psychoanalysis.

*These topics will be explored in depth, including historical context, major contributors, scientific evidence, societal controversies, and lasting modern legacies.*
